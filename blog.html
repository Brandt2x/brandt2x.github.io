Understanding the OWASP Top 10 (2025): What I'm Learning as I Get Into App Security

Posted April 2026

Over the past week, I've been spending more time learning about application security, and one framework that keeps coming up is the OWASP Top 10.

Read more

It's essentially a breakdown of the most common ways web applications get attacked today. What I like about it is that it's practical. It's not just theory, it's based on real vulnerabilities that show up in production environments.

What Stood Out to Me

A few categories really stood out as I went through the 2025 list. Broken Access Control being number one makes sense. A lot of attacks aren't super complex, they're just people accessing things they shouldn't have access to.

Security Misconfiguration is another big one. Something as simple as leaving default settings on or exposing a cloud storage bucket can create a serious risk.

Software Supply Chain Failures is especially relevant now. With how much we rely on third-party packages and CI/CD pipelines, you're not just securing your code anymore, you're trusting everything that comes with it.

Injection is still here, but now it's evolving. It's not just SQL injection, it includes things like command injection and even prompt injection with AI systems.

Cryptographic Failures also stood out. A lot of systems don't fail because encryption doesn't exist, they fail because it's implemented poorly.

How This Connects to My Work

Working in monitoring and alerting, this helped me connect what I'm seeing in logs to actual attack patterns. Before, an alert might just be a failed login or a strange request. Now I'm starting to think:

  • Is this someone testing access controls?
  • Is this an injection attempt?
  • Is this a misconfigured system being exposed?

It's shifting my mindset from just reacting to alerts to actually understanding what could be happening behind them.

Why This Matters Right Now

A lot of these risks aren't edge cases. They're common, and they're actively being exploited. With cloud environments, third-party integrations, and AI becoming more common, the attack surface is only getting bigger. That makes frameworks like the OWASP Top 10 even more relevant.

Where I'm Going Next

Right now, I'm focused on turning this into hands-on experience. I'm building labs, simulating attacks, and using tools like Splunk to detect this kind of activity. The goal is not just to understand these risks, but to be able to find and respond to them in real environments.

Final Thoughts

The OWASP Top 10 is a solid starting point if you're getting into cybersecurity or application security. It gives you a clear view of what actually matters in today's threat landscape and helps you start thinking the way attackers do. For me, it's been one of the first things that made everything start to click.
